Phone storage for secondary schools under MOE's 2026 rules: options compared

What MOE's 2026 rules on phones and smartwatches in secondary schools require, how schools store devices in practice, and a comparison of lockers, collection bags and other storage options.

By Avelogic Payroll & HR TeamUpdated 13 October 20267 min read

Key takeaways

  • From January 2026, secondary school students can't use phones or smartwatches at any time in school hours, including recess and CCAs.
  • MOE asks schools to keep devices in designated storage such as lockers, or in school bags. Each school sets its own arrangements.
  • Schools use school-bag rules, collection bags and phone lockers. The main costs are teacher time at collection and return, and tracking whose phone is where.
  • A numbered, keyless locker that any authorised teacher can open keeps phones accounted for and avoids delays at dismissal.

What MOE's rules say

MOE announced on 30 November 2025 that, from January 2026, the existing ban on phone use during lessons would cover all of school hours in secondary schools. That includes recess, CCAs, and supplementary, enrichment and remedial lessons.

  • Smartwatches are included, because they can send messages and run apps. POSB Smart Buddy watches may still be worn for payments.
  • Devices must be kept in designated storage areas such as lockers, or in school bags.
  • Schools can allow use as an exception where needed.
  • Parents with urgent matters can call the school's General Office, and students can use the General Office phone.

MOE has given school leaders implementation guidelines and examples of good practice, but each school decides its own storage and discipline arrangements.

How schools are doing it

Published school notices show a range of approaches:

  • Designated lockers: phones go in after morning assembly and come out at dismissal. Students caught using a phone have it confiscated.
  • Collection bags first, lockers later: one school started with form teachers collecting phones in labelled bags kept in a central room, which delayed dismissal by 15 to 30 minutes while phones were returned. It planned to move to numbered lockers.
  • Classroom phone lockers: some schools have used them for years.
  • School bags: the simplest option, but parents and teachers note that phones in bags are easy to take out.

What to look for in a phone locker

  • Capacity per class: enough numbered slots for the largest class, ideally matching register numbers.
  • Access control: no keys to lose, and no single teacher who must be present to open it.
  • Phone protection: dividers that keep phones upright and stop scratching.
  • Flexibility: wall-mounted for a fixed classroom, or portable for classes that move.
  • Procurement: a supplier on GeBIZ under the right supply head makes buying simpler.

A simple daily routine

  1. At the start of the day, the form teacher opens the locker and students place phones in their numbered slots.
  2. The teacher checks for empty slots against the register and locks the locker.
  3. The locker stays closed through recess, CCAs and supplementary lessons.
  4. At dismissal, the last-period teacher opens it and students collect their own phones.

Frequently asked questions

Are phones banned in Singapore secondary schools?

From January 2026, secondary school students may not use smartphones or smartwatches during school hours, including recess, CCAs and supplementary lessons. Devices must be kept in designated storage such as lockers, or in school bags.

Are smartwatches included in MOE's rules?

Yes, because they can message and run apps. POSB Smart Buddy watches may still be worn for payments.

How do schools store students' phones?

Approaches include phones kept in bags, collection bags or boxes, lockers with keys or number locks, and keyless phone lockers with numbered slots. Each school chooses its own.

Can a phone locker be bought through GeBIZ?
