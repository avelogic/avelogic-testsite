MOM itemised payslip requirements: the 12 items every payslip needs

Who must issue itemised payslips in Singapore, the 12 items MOM requires, the 3-working-day deadline, record-keeping rules, overtime basics and penalties for non-compliance.

By Avelogic Payroll & HR TeamUpdated 13 October 20266 min read

Key takeaways

  • Every employer must give itemised payslips to employees covered by the Employment Act.
  • Issue the payslip with the salary, or within 3 working days of paying it.
  • MOM lists 12 items. Leave out any that don't apply.
  • Keep salary records for the latest 2 years, and for 1 year after an employee leaves.

Who must issue itemised payslips

Since 1 April 2016, every employer must issue itemised payslips to all employees covered by the Employment Act. That covers anyone on a contract of service, local or foreign, full-time, part-time, temporary or on contract. It doesn't cover seafarers, domestic workers, or civil servants and statutory board employees, who have their own rules.

When to issue them

  • Give the payslip together with the salary payment.
  • If that's not possible, issue it within 3 working days of paying the salary.
  • When employment ends, issue the payslip with the final salary payment.
  • If you pay several times in a month, one consolidated payslip is fine.

Payslips can be soft or hard copies, and even handwritten payslips are acceptable.

The 12 items a payslip must show

#ItemNotes
1Employer's full name
2Employee's full name
3Date(s) of payment
4Basic salaryFor hourly, daily or piece-rated staff: the basic rate and total hours, days or pieces worked
5Start and end date of the salary period
6AllowancesFixed and ad hoc, e.g. transport
7Other additional paymentse.g. bonus, rest-day pay, public holiday pay
8DeductionsFixed and ad hoc, e.g. employee CPF, no-pay leave
9Overtime hours worked
10Overtime pay
11Start and end date of the overtime periodOnly if different from the salary period
12Net salary paid

Source: Ministry of Manpower (MOM)

If an item doesn't apply, such as overtime for a manager who isn't entitled to it, you can leave it out.

How long to keep salary records

Salary records cover the same information as the payslip. Keep them for the latest 2 years for current employees. When someone leaves, keep their last 2 years of records for 1 year after they leave.

Overtime basics that affect payslips

  • Overtime is paid at no less than 1.5 times the hourly basic rate.
  • It applies to workmen earning up to $4,500 basic a month, and to other employees earning up to $2,600 basic a month.
  • For non-workmen, the overtime rate is capped at the $2,600 salary level, which works out to $13.60 an hour.
  • Overtime is limited to 72 hours a month unless MOM grants an exemption.

What happens if you don't comply

On a first complaint, MOM usually guides the employer to start issuing proper payslips within a month. If complaints keep coming, MOM can send caution letters and impose an administrative penalty of up to $400 for each repeated breach. MOM receives about 670 payslip complaints a year.

Frequently asked questions

Is an itemised payslip compulsory in Singapore?

Yes, for all employees covered by the Employment Act, since 1 April 2016.

How soon must a payslip be issued?

With the salary payment, or within 3 working days of paying the salary.

Can payslips be sent electronically?

Yes. MOM accepts soft copies as well as hard copies, including handwritten payslips.

How long must salary records be kept?

For the latest 2 years for current employees, and for 1 year after an employee leaves.
